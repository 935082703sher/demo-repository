# Demo 3 Stage 3A Legal Assumptions

## Confirmed engineering requirements

- No Stage 3A action registers or submits an official appeal.
- No case number, official status, department assignment, decision, or deadline exists.
- Applicant type, appeal kind, service category, and subcategory are separate.
- Unknown classifications require clarification or review.
- Consent is explicit and bound to the exact version, hash, notice, language, and time.
- Sensitive values are references only in the ordinary workflow model.

These are project safety controls, not statements of Uzbek law.

## Provisional engineering assumptions

- The four appeal-kind identifiers are useful technical placeholders.
- Existing Stage 2 subcategories are sufficient for initial type and transition tests.
- Requirements can eventually be supplied as governed versioned profiles.
- A future secure system can issue opaque references without exposing storage details.
- A human-review requirement should stop automatic progression.

Every assumption remains pending legal, content, privacy, and architecture review.

## Unresolved legal questions

- authoritative definitions and translations of application, proposal, and complaint;
- which fields are legally required for each applicant/appeal/category combination;
- representation and authority requirements;
- consent wording, withdrawal, evidence, and legal effect;
- applicable deadlines, tariff meaning, eligibility rules, and legal remedies;
- accessibility, minor/vulnerable-person, and emergency handling duties;
- whether and when legal summaries may be automated.

## Content-owner decisions required

- controlled subcategories outside IMEI/MNP;
- approved clarification and missing-field prompts;
- review triggers and safe non-sensitive summary vocabulary;
- approved source/profile ownership and review cadence;
- Uzbek, Russian, and English labels and public messages.

## Privacy decisions required

- lawful basis and purpose limitation;
- minimum ordinary and secure fields;
- retention, deletion, access, audit, encryption, residency, and processors;
- treatment of attachments, logs, analytics, backups, and incident response;
- privacy-notice versions and consent evidence.

## Identity-verification decisions required

- supported applicant identity methods;
- legal-entity and representative authority checks;
- verification provider and assurance level;
- failure, mismatch, revocation, and manual-review handling;
- what verification metadata, if any, may enter the ordinary workflow.
